01
Navigation · Conversion

One product per range, shade as a swatch. Not one product per shade.

Today "Cocoa Seamless", "Chocolate Seamless" and "Jet Black Seamless" are three separate products. Multiply that by every shade and every range and the collection page becomes a wall of near-identical cards.

The problem

  • Products start below the fold. On desktop the collection opens with a full-width banner and four paragraphs of SEO copy. The first product card is a full screen down.
  • Choice overload before understanding. Page 1 of Clip In Hair Extensions shows 16 cards for 4 ranges (Seamless, Double Wefted Lace, Invisible Deluxe, Volumizer). A new visitor has to work out what the ranges are by reading titles like "Brown Black - One Weft Volumizer Seamless Clip In Remy Human Hair Extensions".
  • Reviews are fragmented. Cocoa Seamless shows 9 Yotpo reviews. The Seamless range as a whole will have hundreds. Each shade page looks like a niche product instead of a bestseller.
  • SEO and ranking signals are diluted across dozens of URLs for the same product.
  • Shade comparison is hard. To compare Cocoa and Chocolate the shopper opens two tabs.
Source: foxylocks.com/clip-in-hair-extensions/ and the Cocoa Seamless product page HTML, fetched 15 Oct 2026. 160 card elements in the collection markup; reviews_count: 9 on the PDP.

The fix

  • Restructure to 4 to 6 range products with shade as a variant swatch. Collection page becomes "pick your range", product page becomes "pick your shade and length".
  • Aggregate reviews per range so every PDP shows the full count and the shade-specific photos filter underneath.
  • Shade-first entry path kept for people who already know their colour: "Shop by colour" lands them on the range page with the swatch preselected.
  • Collection filters that matter: goal (length vs volume), shade family, weight, price. Hide the ones nobody uses.

02
Conversion · Returns

Put shade confidence at the point of decision, not in the top bar.

The biggest reason someone hesitates on a £100 hair extension is "will it match?". Foxy Locks already has the answer (colour match service, quiz, 24 shades), but it lives in the USP strip and a nav link, not next to the add-to-basket button.

The problem

  • The first thing on the PDP is a popup. "Unlock Your Private Offer" covers the buy box before the shopper has seen the price, with a cookie banner underneath and the promo bar above. Three interruptions before one decision.
  • The match tools are small and off the path. "Find My Match" is a text link beside the swatches; the quiz is a page in the nav; "Colour matching available" sits in the grey USP bar. None of them is framed as the answer to "will it match me?" at the moment that question is asked.
  • Length and weight options read as codes. 14" 120g, 16" 150g, 18" 180g, 20" 160g, 20" 200g, 22" 230g, 24" 280g. Two different 20" options with no explanation of which to pick.
  • Wrong-shade anxiety becomes returns, which cost margin and CS time (Gorgias is in the stack, so the team is already absorbing this).
Source: Cocoa Seamless PDP markup, 15 Oct 2026 (7 size options, "Free UK delivery on orders over £70", Klarna and Clearpay messaging present, match tools absent from the buy box).

The fix

  • "Not sure of your shade?" module in the buy box: 60-second quiz or photo upload, returns a recommended shade and preselects it.
  • Free or £3 shade swatch add-on ("Try the shade first, credit it back on your order") for the hesitant segment.
  • Shade swap guarantee stated under the button: "Wrong shade? Swap free within 30 days." One line removes the biggest objection.
  • Human-readable options: group by goal ("Natural · 16" 150g", "Full glam · 22" 230g") and flag the most popular pick.
  • Real customer photos filtered by shade under the gallery, pulled from the reviews you already collect.

03
AOV

Sell the routine, not the product. Give every basket a reason to grow.

Free UK delivery kicks in at £70. The cheapest full set is £70. So the one incentive that reliably grows baskets is already spent before the shopper adds anything else, and the care range (Silk In A Bottle, accessories) is left to be discovered on its own.

The problem

  • Threshold does no work. A £70 threshold against £70 to £120 hero products means no "add £X more" moment on most orders.
  • Care and accessories are a separate aisle. Hair Care Products and Accessories are nav items, not attach-rate drivers on the extension PDP or in the basket.
  • No step-up nudge on length. The 7 size options are priced silently; nothing tells the shopper that most people go one step longer.
  • Post-purchase is empty. After paying, the highest-intent moment of the whole journey offers nothing.
Source: PDP markup ("Free UK delivery on orders over £70", options 14" to 24"), homepage bestsellers carousel including Silk In A Bottle duo, 15 Oct 2026.

The fix

  • Move the threshold to roughly 1.3× the hero price (£120 to £130) and show a progress bar in the basket drawer. Pair it with a care product that closes the gap.
  • "Complete your routine" bundle on the PDP: extensions + Silk In A Bottle duo + heat protect + storage case, saving 10 to 15%. One click, one price.
  • Step-up pricing shown as a delta: "+£20 for 22 inch, chosen by 42% of customers".
  • Post-purchase one-click offer: care duo or a second shade for highlights at a thank-you-page price, charged to the same payment. No re-entry of card details.
  • Loyalty points visible in basket: "You'll earn 240 points on this order" turns the rewards programme into a live incentive.

04
Navigation · Brand

Let the homepage sell the result. Let the nav sell the route.

Right now the first screen is a 40% off banner, a sale hero, a 7-item nav, a 6-item USP bar and a cookie notice. It works for a returning buyer with a code. It does nothing for the person who has never worn extensions and does not know where to start.

The problem

  • Discount-led first impression for a brand positioned as luxury, "trusted since 2009", featured in Vogue and Glamour. The code trains visitors to wait for the next sale.
  • Four stacked bars before content. Promo bar, header, nav, USP strip. On mobile that is most of the screen before the hero.
  • Nav is organised by product type (Clip In, Tape In, Ponytails, Lashes, Hair Care, Gift Sets, Outlet). New visitors think in goals: "I want length", "I want volume", "what colour am I?".
  • Education is buried. Video tutorials, the quiz, colour match and the blog are all strong, and all several clicks deep.
Source: homepage screenshot and markup, 15 Oct 2026. 40+ distinct links in header and footer navigation.

The fix

  • Hero that sells transformation: before/after or real customer video (Tolstoy is already installed), one headline about the result, two CTAs: "Find my shade" and "Shop bestsellers". Promo moves to a slim bar.
  • Nav by intent: Shop (by type · by goal · by colour), Find your shade, Learn (tutorials, quiz, blog), Help. A visual mega menu with one image per range.
  • One trust bar, not two. Merge the USP strip into the promo bar: "Free UK delivery over £120 · Free shade swaps · Hair Awards 2025".
  • Homepage sections in buying order: shade finder, bestsellers by range, real results, how to apply, reviews, loyalty.

05
Conversion · Trust

Finish stronger: accelerated checkout and one voice of proof.

The last two things a shopper sees are the checkout form and the social proof around it. Today the checkout is a standard BigCommerce flow and the proof is split between two review platforms that never add up.

The problem

  • No Shop Pay. Returning customers and the 150m+ people with a Shop Pay wallet re-type address and card details. Mobile drop-off lives here.
  • Two review systems, half the impact. Trustpilot runs in the header, Yotpo on products. Neither shows the full number anywhere, and the per-shade split (idea 1) makes it worse.
  • Award and press credibility is passive. "Hair Awards 2025", Vogue, Glamour and Cosmopolitan sit in corners rather than beside the price and the pay button.
  • Currency is detected, not chosen. Our fetch was served USD pricing; a UK shopper on a VPN or travelling sees the same. Worth checking how often this happens.
Source: homepage and PDP markup, 15 Oct 2026 (BigCommerce theme, Trustpilot header script, Yotpo widgets, PDP price rendered as $211.50). Shop Pay conversion figures are Shopify's published data: shopify.com/uk/shop-pay.

The fix

  • Shop Pay plus Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal and Klarna as one express row on the PDP, basket and checkout. Shopify's published data puts Shop Pay checkout conversion up to 50% higher than guest checkout.
  • Pick one review platform and show the total everywhere: header, PDP buy box, basket, checkout. Keep Trustpilot for Google stars if it earns them; stop showing both to shoppers.
  • Proof in the pay path: award badge, press logos and "4.8 from 12,000 reviews" in the checkout sidebar.
  • Explicit market selector and localised pricing per country rather than silent geo-detection.
